**1. Emergency Incidents and Responses**

**Quotes:**
- "coordinate the city's response to all emergency conditions and potential incidents which require a multi-agency response, including but not limited to severe weather, threats from natural hazards and natural disasters, power and other public service outages, labor unrest other than the keeping of the peace, water main breaks, transportation and transit incidents, hazardous substance discharges, building collapses, aviation disasters, explosions, acts of terrorism and such other emergency conditions and incidents which affect public health and safety;" (§497.a)
- "monitor on a constant basis all potential emergency conditions and potential incidents which may require a multi-agency response;" (§497.b)
- "operate an emergency operations center to assist the city in managing emergency conditions and potential incidents that may require a multi-agency response;" (§497.g)
- "The department shall be the lead agency in the coordination and facilitation of resources in incidents involving public safety and health, including incidents which may involve acts of terrorism." (§498.a)

**Why:** To effectively coordinate and manage emergency responses, the agency must track each incident and its multi-agency response.

**One row represents:** A single emergency condition or potential incident requiring multi-agency response.

**Covers:** All emergency conditions and potential incidents that require or may require a multi-agency response, from initial monitoring to full resolution.

**Columns:**
- Incident ID
- Incident Status (e.g., Monitored, Active, Resolved, Closed)
- Incident Type (e.g., Severe Weather, Natural Hazard, Power Outage, Water Main Break, Transportation Incident, Hazardous Substance Discharge, Building Collapse, Aviation Disaster, Explosion, Act of Terrorism, Other)
- Incident Sub-Type (e.g., Hurricane, Blizzard, Flood, Earthquake, Cyber Attack)
- Date/Time Detected
- Date/Time Declared Emergency (if applicable)
- Date/Time EOC Activated (if applicable)
- Date/Time EOC Deactivated (if applicable)
- Date/Time Resolution Achieved
- Date/Time Incident Closed
- Geographic Location (e.g., address, coordinates, borough, zone)
- Lead Agency (EMD or other, if delegated)
- List of Responding Agencies (e.g., Fire, Police, Health, Sanitation, Transportation)
- Resources Deployed (e.g., personnel count, equipment type, specific assets)
- Impact Assessment (e.g., fatalities, injuries, property damage estimate, service disruptions)
- Public Information Dissemination Channels Used (e.g., social media, emergency alerts, press conferences)
- Incident Commander/Lead EMD Official (links to Dataset 14)
- Related Emergency Plan ID (links to Dataset 2)
- Mayoral Declaration Status (Yes/No, Date)
- Federal/State Aid Requested (Yes/No, Date, Type)
- Federal/State Aid Received (Yes/No, Date, Amount)

**2. Emergency Plans**

**Quotes:**
- "prepare plans for responding to emergency conditions and potential incidents, including but not limited to plans for the implementation of such emergency orders as may be approved by the mayor to protect public safety and facilitate the rapid response and mobilization of agencies and resources;" (§497.d)
- "coordinate with all city agencies to ensure that all such agencies develop, implement and periodically review citywide emergency plans;" (§497.j)
- "coordinate New York city's civil defense effort in accordance with the provisions of the Defense Emergency Act of New York state and the city's civil defense emergency operations plan, as such plan may be amended from time to time;" (§497.m)
- "No later than two years after the effective date of the local law that created this subdivision, and every two years thereafter, the department shall convene relevant agencies to review citywide emergency plans coordinated by the department." (§498.b)
- "Following each review conducted pursuant to subdivision b of this section, the department shall publish on its website a list of citywide emergency plans coordinated by the department and a summary of each such plan, including but not limited to information on the roles and responsibilities of relevant agencies and other partners." (§498.c)

**Why:** To manage, track, and ensure the existence and currency of all emergency plans, both EMD's and citywide.

**One row represents:** A single emergency plan.

**Covers:** All emergency plans prepared by EMD, citywide emergency plans coordinated by EMD, and the city's civil defense emergency operations plan.

**Columns:**
- Plan ID
- Plan Name
- Plan Type (e.g., Citywide, Departmental, Hazard-Specific, Civil Defense)
- Lead Department/Agency for Plan Development
- Date Created
- Last Review Date (links to Dataset 5)
- Next Scheduled Review Date
- Approval Status (e.g., Draft, Approved, Under Revision)
- Approval Date
- Version Number
- Scope/Applicability (e.g., citywide, specific borough, specific hazard)
- Key Agencies Involved in Plan (list of Agency IDs)
- Roles and Responsibilities Summary (text field, but could be a link to a separate table of roles)
- Publicly Available (Yes/No)
- Publication Date (if publicly available)
- Mayoral Determination for Non-Publication (Yes/No, Date, Reason if Yes)
- URL to Plan Summary (if published)

**5. Citywide Emergency Plan Review Cycles**

**Quotes:**
- "No later than two years after the effective date of the local law that created this subdivision, and every two years thereafter, the department shall convene relevant agencies to review citywide emergency plans coordinated by the department." (§498.b)
- "Following each review conducted pursuant to subdivision b of this section, the department shall publish on its website a list of citywide emergency plans coordinated by the department and a summary of each such plan, including but not limited to information on the roles and responsibilities of relevant agencies and other partners. Publication of summaries concerning a particular plan shall not be required where the mayor has determined that it would impede the city's ability to plan for and respond to threats to security including terrorism and cyber attacks; however, in such instances the department shall disclose the existence of such plans and identity relevant agencies and other partners involved in implementation of such plans." (§498.c)

**Why:** To ensure compliance with the biennial review mandate and track the outcomes and publications related to these reviews.

**One row represents:** A single biennial review cycle of citywide emergency plans.

**Covers:** All mandated biennial review cycles of citywide emergency plans coordinated by EMD.

**Columns:**
- Review Cycle ID
- Review Start Date
- Review End Date
- Mandated Review Due Date
- Status (e.g., In Progress, Completed, Overdue)
- EMD Lead Coordinator ID (links to Dataset 14)
- Participating Agencies in Review (list of Agency IDs)
- Number of Plans Reviewed
- Date List of Plans Published
- URL to Published List of Plans
- Date Summaries Published (if applicable)
- URL to Published Plan Summaries (if applicable)
- Mayoral Determination for Non-Publication of Summaries (Yes/No, Date, Reason if Yes)
- Plans with Non-Published Summaries (list of Plan IDs from Dataset 2)
- Public Comment Period Start Date (links to Dataset 11)
- Public Comment Period End Date (links to Dataset 11)
