**Data Systems for Program Implementation**

**1. Agency Vision and Objectives:**
The Police Department's vision is to ensure public safety and order through law enforcement, crime prevention, and emergency response. Key objectives include:
*   Effective civil and criminal enforcement of laws and regulations.
*   Efficient and timely response to 911 emergency calls and incidents.
*   Prevention of crime and delinquency, particularly among minors.
*   Maintenance of department facilities and equipment.
*   Accountable and disciplined police force.
*   Compliance with legal and reporting requirements, including those related to bias-based profiling, use of force, property management, and inter-agency cooperation.
*   Promotion of community relations and public trust.
*   Specialized services for victims of domestic violence, sexual crimes, and human trafficking.
*   Safe and secure school environments.

**What counts as a win:**
*   Reduced crime rates (major felonies, specific offenses).
*   Improved 911 response times.
*   High clearance rates for serious crimes.
*   Effective management of personnel, including appropriate discipline and training.
*   Accurate and transparent reporting of police activities and data.
*   Successful recovery and disposition of seized property.
*   Minimized instances of bias-based profiling and excessive force.
*   Effective support for vulnerable populations (minors, domestic violence victims, individuals with autism spectrum disorder).
*   Full compliance with all legal and reporting mandates.

**How to know it's a win:**
Success is measured through the various reports mandated by the text (e.g., 911 operational time analysis, crime status reports, use of force reports, property clerk reports, personnel deployment reports, domestic violence/hate crime statistics, school activity reports, traffic data, investigative encounter reports). These reports provide quantitative metrics and qualitative insights into departmental performance, compliance, and impact on the community.

**2. Agency Capacities:**
*   **Human Resources:** A large police force with various ranks (Captains, Lieutenants, Sergeants, Police Officers, Surgeons, Veterinarian), specialized roles (Detectives, Juvenile Bureau members, Superintendent of Buildings, Property Clerk), and civilian staff (School Crossing Guards, Traffic and Parking Enforcement employees, Special Patrolmen, Auxiliary Police, School Safety Agents). Extensive training programs are mandated.
*   **Technical Resources:** 911 emergency assistance system, state and federal databases (e-JusticeNY), body-worn cameras, centralized electronic system for parking permits, surveillance technology (including facial recognition), internal tracking systems for officer activity and data sharing.
*   **Financial Resources:** Appropriations for contingent expenses, salaries, equipment (horses, automotive equipment, uniforms, badges, opioid antagonists, AEDs), pension funds, workers' compensation, rewards, and funding for various programs and services.
*   **Physical Resources:** Precincts, station houses, substations, department buildings, vehicles, boats, firearms, communication systems (radio).

**3. Agency Obligations (Overhead):**
*   **Personnel Management:** Recruitment, appointment, promotion, salary administration, pension contributions, workers' compensation, discipline, suspension, termination, re-hearings, managing resignations and leaves, ensuring certifications (e.g., CPR/AED).
*   **Facilities Management:** Establishing and maintaining precincts, station houses, substations, providing accommodations for detainees (especially women), managing department buildings and mechanical forces.
*   **Equipment Management:** Procurement, maintenance, and tracking of uniforms, badges, official identification cards, vehicles, boats, horses, firearms, opioid antagonists, AEDs, body-worn cameras, and other surveillance technology.
*   **Financial Management:** Budgeting, managing contingent expenses, processing payroll, administering pension funds, handling rewards, managing seized property funds, tracking overtime spending.
*   **Legal & Regulatory Compliance:** Enforcing civil and criminal laws, executing warrants, complying with health code enforcement, environmental compliance, adhering to bias-based profiling prohibitions, implementing consent search guidelines, child-sensitive arrest policies, DNA collection consent rules, radio communication policies, and internal disciplinary matrix.
*   **Reporting & Transparency:** Quarterly and annual reports to the City Council, Mayor, Public Advocate, state officials, and public website postings on a wide range of activities (crime statistics, 911 response, personnel deployment, use of force, property seizures, traffic data, school activity, domestic violence, hate crimes, immigration detainers, marijuana enforcement, opioid antagonist use, surveillance technology, investigative encounters, youth programs).
*   **Inter-agency Coordination:** Cooperating with the Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, Board of Education, Human Resources Administration, Department of Social Services, Department of Transportation, District Attorneys, Civilian Complaint Review Board, Office of Children and Family Services, Office of Nightlife, Commissioner of Investigation, and MTA.
*   **Training & Development:** Developing and delivering training programs for recruits and existing staff on various topics including elder abuse, harassment/sexual assault sensitivity, special victims investigations, child-sensitive arrests, autism spectrum disorder safety, and identity theft.
*   **Public Services:** Administering voluntary fingerprinting programs for school children, providing information on reduced fare programs, and developing youth programs.

**Quotes:**
*   "Investigative encounter. The term “investigative encounter” means an interaction between a member of the department and a member of the public for a law enforcement or investigative purpose. The term does not include a casual conversation or interaction between a member of the department and a member of the public unless such conversation or interaction is based on or, in the course of such conversation or interaction, an officer develops: an objective credible reason to approach; a founded suspicion that such member of the public has engaged in or will engage in criminal activity; a reasonable suspicion that such member of the public has committed, is committing, or is about to commit a crime; or a reasonable cause to believe that an offense is being or has been committed." (§ 14-196.a)
*   "Level I encounter. The term “level I encounter” means an investigative encounter in which a member of the department requests information from a member of the public based on an objective credible reason for such encounter, and need not necessarily require any suspicion of criminal activity." (§ 14-196.a)
*   "Level II encounter. The term “level II encounter” means an investigative encounter in which a member of the department requests information from a member of the public based on a founded suspicion that such member of the public has engaged in or will engage in criminal activity, and during which a reasonable person would feel free to leave." (§ 14-196.a)
*   "Level III encounter. The term “level III encounter” means an investigative encounter in which a member of the department requests information from a member of the public based on a reasonable suspicion that such member of the public has committed, is committing, or is about to commit a crime, and during which a reasonable person would not feel free to leave." (§ 14-196.a)

The total number of level III encounters." (§ 14-196.b)

**30. Child Care Arrangements (Arrested Caregiver)**
**Quotes:**
*   "Providing an arrested caregiver the opportunity to speak with a child who is present, prior to such caregiver being transported to a police facility. If such an opportunity is not practicable, having a police officer explain to such child, using age appropriate language, that that such child did nothing wrong and that the child will be safe and cared for." (§ 14-181.b.5)
*   "Providing objects that provide comfort to the child of an arrested caregiver, such as toys, clothing, blankets, photographs, or food." (§ 14-181.b.6)
*   "Ascertaining any medical, behavioral, or psychological conditions or required treatments of the child of an arrested caregiver and securing any required medication for such child." (§ 14-181.b.7)
*   "Permitting an arrested caregiver a reasonable opportunity, including providing access to telephone numbers stored in a mobile telephone or other location, to make alternate arrangements for the care of a child, including a child who is not present at the scene of the arrest, and to provide the department or a partner organization with contact information of a preferred alternate caregiver." (§ 14-181.b.8)
*   "When an alternate caregiver is unable to arrive at the scene of arrest within a reasonable amount of time, making a referral to a partner organization or bringing the child to the borough's child advocacy center. If no alternate caregiver is identified or the identified caregiver does not arrive in a reasonable amount of time, or if the child advocacy center or partner organization is unavailable, notifying the New York state central register of the office of children and family services and notifying the administration for children's services borough field office or the administration for children's services' emergency children's services program or its equivalent." (§ 14-181.b.9)
*   "Not notifying the administration for children's services and the state central register of the office of children and family services unless required by law." (§ 14-181.b.10)
*   "After arrest and prior to arraignment in a criminal court, affording arrested caregivers adequate opportunities to make phone calls to check on the status and well-being of a child." (§ 14-181.b.11)
*   "After an arrest and prior to arraignment in a criminal court, offering to make a referral to a partner organization that could provide appropriate services to the child and, with the caregiver's consent, making such referral." (§ 14-181.b.12)
*   "Reporting. Within 30 days of January 1, 2020, January 1, 2021, and January 1, 2022, the department shall submit to the speaker of the council and post on its website a report on the number of instances requiring a referral to a partner organization or transportation of the child to a child advocacy center pursuant to paragraph 9 of subdivision b of this section." (§ 14-181.d)
